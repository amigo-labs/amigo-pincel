The History of Printing

Printing with movable type changed the way people shared knowledge. Before the
printing press, books were copied by hand, which took menths and made every copy
expensive. Within a few decades, printed books became common in many European
cities, and the number of readers grew quickly.

Early printers mixed their own ink from soot, varnish and egg white. They cast letters
from an alloy of lead, tin and antimony, which melted at a low temperature and did not
shrink when it cooled. A skilled team could print several hundred sheets in a single day.
