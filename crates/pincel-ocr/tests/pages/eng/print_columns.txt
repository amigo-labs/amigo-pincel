Notes on Colour and Light

Every colour we see is light that an object
reflects. A red apple absorbs most of the
light that falls on it and reflects mainly the
red part of the spectrum back to our eyes.

Painters mix pigments, which subtract
light, while screens add light. That is why
mixing paints gives darker colours, but
mixing coloured light on a screen gives
brighter ones.

Printers use cyan, magenta, yellow and
black inks. Screens use red, green and blue
pixels. Converting an image between the
two systems always changes some of its
colours.

A good monitor should be calibrated at
least once a year, so that the colours on the
screen match the colours of the printed
result.
